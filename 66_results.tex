\subsection{Parameter Convergence and Estimation}

Network optimization is driven by the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-3}$ \cite{serrano_physics-informed_2024}. To systematically evaluate the learning characteristics of the PINN, various penalty weighting coefficients ($\lambda$) for the composite physics loss were tested.

The experimental flight sequence initiates at $t = 0$ s with a vertical ascent to a baseline hover. The system stabilizes at an altitude of 3 meters for an initial 5-second window, allowing the physical state to settle before dynamic maneuvers commence. To rigorously excite the system's rotational dynamics and test the estimator under varying degrees of coupling, the rigid-frame is subjected to three distinct spatial trajectories: a figure-8, a circular path, and a uniaxial "lawnmower" motion.

For the sake of brevity in the manuscript, we define only the desired 3D position vector $\mathbf{p}_d(t)$ for each maneuver. The corresponding feedforward velocity $\mathbf{v}_d(t)$ and acceleration $\mathbf{a}_d(t)$ setpoints utilized by the geometric controller are computed via exact analytical time differentiation of $\mathbf{p}_d(t)$.

The Figure-8 trajectory incorporates a constant radius ($R = 8$ m), an angular frequency ($\omega = 0.10$ rad/s), and a sinusoidal vertical variation ($z_v$) to simultaneously stress the thrust allocation bounds:\begin{equation}\mathbf{p}_d(t) = \begin{bmatrix}R \sin(\omega t) \\frac{R}{2} \sin(2\omega t) z_0 + z_v \sin(\omega t)\end{bmatrix}.\end{equation}

The Circular trajectory features a tighter radius ($R = 5$ m) and faster angular frequency ($\omega = 0.15$ rad/s), retaining the continuous altitude variation:\begin{equation}\mathbf{p}_d(t) = \begin{bmatrix}R \sin(\omega t) R (1 - \cos(\omega t)) z_0 + z_v \sin(\omega t)\end{bmatrix}.\end{equation}

The Lawnmower maneuver isolates longitudinal dynamics by commanding forward-backward motion exclusively along the world X-axis at an elevated, constant altitude of $z_0 = 5$ m. For a single maneuver phase with an amplitude $A = 2.0$ m and duration $T_{phase} = 5.0$ s, the trajectory is defined as:

% path trajectory
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}d(t) = \begin{bmatrix}
\frac{A}{2} (1 - \cos(\omega_L t)) \
0 \
z_0
\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $\omega_L = \frac{2\pi}{T{phase}}$ represents the governing phase angular frequency.

The trajectory tracking results in Figure. \ref{fig:combined_states} highlight the advantage of incorporating physics constraints into the estimation architecture. As depicted in the top row, the data-only baseline configuration struggles to maintain the desired path, exhibiting significant drift and steady-state error across the Figure-8, lawnmower, and circular maneuvers. Conversely, the bottom row demonstrates that the proposed physics-informed approach tightly adheres to the reference trajectories. By embedding the physical system's dynamic model into the loss function, the system successfully compensates for complex dynamic coupling and payload influences during flight, resulting in a highly stable tracking response.

These visual improvements are supported by the quantitative steady-state estimation errors presented in Table. \ref{tab:ep_res_all}. Relying strictly on the data loss ($\lambda_{1,2,3} = 1,0,0$) yields a high trajectory RMSE, such as 0.8863 m for the Figure-8 maneuver and 0.8528 m for the circular path. Activating the physics constraints ($\lambda_{1,2,3} = 1,1,1$) drastically reduces these tracking errors to 0.3650 m and 0.2315 m, respectively.

Furthermore, the parameter estimation data reveals a fundamental limitation of the data-only baseline, it completely fails to identify the spatial center-of-mass offsets, returning $0.0000$ m for both $\hat{d}_x$ and $\hat{d}_y$ across all trajectories. The proposed physics-informed configurations successfully estimate these spatial offsets alongside the payload mass ($\hat{m}$) and inertia tensor components ($\hat{J}_{xx}, \hat{J}_{yy}, \hat{J}_{zz}$). This confirms that the physics-based loss terms are essential for achieving accurate online parameter estimation and robust rigid-frame payload transportation.
