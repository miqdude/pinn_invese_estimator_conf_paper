\subsection{Physics-Informed Parameter Estimation}
Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) represent a paradigm shift in scientific machine learning by seamlessly embedding known physical laws into the deep learning optimization process. Unlike purely data-driven models that rely exclusively on large empirical datasets to learn system behaviors implicitly, PINNs utilize governing Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) or Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) as explicit regularizers within their loss function \cite{raissi2019physics,mojgani_kolmogorov_2023}.

The network typically consists of fully connected Artificial Neural Network (ANN) layers parameterized by weights and biases $\theta$ \cite{raissi2019physics}. To illustrate the fundamental mechanism, consider a general dynamical system governed by an ODE of the form $\mathcal{F}(u, \dot{u}, \ddot{u}, t) = 0$. The PINN acts as a surrogate model, taking the independent variable time $t$ as its sole input and outputting a predicted system state $\hat{u}(t)$:

\begin{equation}
    \hat{u}(t) = \mathcal{N}_{\theta}(t).
\end{equation}

During training, the network minimizes a composite loss function $\mathcal{L}_{total}$ that balances empirical observation with physical consistency. The data loss ($\mathcal{L}_{data}$) anchors the network to reality by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the network's predictions $\hat{u}(t)$ measured state $u_{true}$ multiply with a scalar hyperparameter $\lambda_1$ at specific time steps $t_i$:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{data} = \lambda_1 \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \left\lVert \hat{u}(t_i) - u_{true}(t_i) \right\rVert^2 .
\end{equation}

Simultaneously, the network evaluates the physical validity of its predictions. Rather than relying on numerical approximation, PINNs leverage Automatic Differentiation (Autograd) to compute exact analytical derivatives of the output with respect to the input (e.g., $\dot{\hat{u}} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{N}_{\theta}}{\partial t}$). These derivatives are substituted back into the governing equation to form a physics residual $R(t) = \mathcal{F}(\hat{u}, \dot{\hat{u}}, \ddot{\hat{u}}, t)$. The physics loss ($\mathcal{L}_{physics}$) penalizes any deviation from zero across $M$ collocation points:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{physics} = \lambda_2 \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^{M} \left\lVert R(t_j) \right\rVert^2 .
\end{equation}

The total loss is calculated as a weighted sum of these objectives, $\mathcal{L}_{total} = \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{data} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{physics}$. By minimizing this composite function, the network learns to predict states that not only match the data but also strictly obey the underlying physical mechanics of the system \cite{mojgani_kolmogorov_2023}.
