\section{System Description}
\subsection{System Modeling}

The rigid-frame vehicle consists of a 0.8 kg, $1 \times 1$ m square board with four 1.5 kg quadrotors rigidly mounted at its corners, displaced by $\pm 0.5$ m from the geometric center. We define an inertial world frame $\mathcal{W}$ with an upward-pointing Z-axis, and a primary body frame $\mathcal{B}$ anchored at the board's geometric center. Within $\mathcal{B}$, the positive X-axis designates the forward direction of the vehicle, and the positive Y-axis points to the left, completing a right-handed coordinate system. At the identity orientation (level and unrotated), the axes of $\mathcal{B}$ perfectly align with $\mathcal{W}$. Each quadrotor is modeled as an ideal actuator producing direct thrust, and each maintains a local coordinate frame that is strictly parallel to $\mathcal{B}$. Due to the rigid attachments, there is no relative rotation between the drones and the central board; their alignment differs from $\mathcal{B}$ purely by a fixed spatial translation in the body's XY plane. The vehicle's global position in $\mathcal{W}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{r} = [x, y, z]^T$, and its orientation is parameterized by the Euler angles $\mathbf{\eta} = [\phi, \theta, \psi]^T$.

The MAS employs a centralized control architecture derived from \cite{mellinger2013trajectory}. The composite rigid body is commanded via a unified control wrench $\boldsymbol{\tau}$, defined as:

\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} = [F_B, M_{xB}, M_{yB}, M_{zB}]^T,
\end{equation}

\noindent where $F_B$ represents the total collective thrust acting along the frame's $z$-axis, while $M_{xB}$, $M_{yB}$, and $M_{zB}$ denote the respective desired roll, pitch, and yaw control moments applied about the frame which relates to individual quadrotor thrust inputs $\mathbf{u} = [F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4]^T$ via the allocation matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tau}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} = A \mathbf{u},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    A =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
        y_1 & y_2 & y_3 & y_4 \\
        -x_1 & -x_2 & -x_3 & -x_4 \\
        c_1 & c_2 & c_3 & c_4
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

The rows of $A$ denote total thrust, roll moment, pitch moment, and yaw reactive torque, respectively.

\subsection{Dynamic Thrust Allocation}
A payload shifts from the body central frame, altering the effective moment arms. Let $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{\text{cog}} = [\hat{x}_{\text{cog}}, \hat{y}_{\text{cog}}]^T$ denote the estimated offset. Redefining the lever arms as $\Delta x_i = x_i - \hat{x}_{\text{cog}}$ and $\Delta y_i = y_i - \hat{y}_{\text{cog}}$, the allocation matrix adapts dynamically:

\begin{equation}
    A_{\text{final}} = \begin{bmatrix}
    1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
    \Delta y_1 & \Delta y_2 & \Delta y_3 & \Delta y_4 \\
    -\Delta x_1 & -\Delta x_2 & -\Delta x_3 & -\Delta x_4 \\
    c_1 & c_2 & c_3 & c_4
    \end{bmatrix}. \label{eq:allocation_matrix}
\end{equation}

Control inputs are computed via the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse to handle transient rank deficiencies:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u} = A_{\text{final}}^{+} \boldsymbol{\tau}. \label{eq:final_u}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Centralized Control Design}
Based on \cite{mellinger2013trajectory}, the controller separates position and attitude tracking. Position and velocity errors define commanded acceleration $\mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{cmd}$:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_p = \mathbf{r}_{des} - \mathbf{r}, \quad \mathbf{e}_v = \mathbf{\dot{r}}_{des} - \mathbf{\dot{r}},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{cmd} = K_p \mathbf{e}_p + K_d \mathbf{e}_v + \mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{\text{ff}}.
\end{equation}

Desired roll ($\phi^{des}$), pitch ($\theta^{des}$), and total thrust ($F_B^{des}$) are extracted via small-angle approximation:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi^{des} &= \frac{1}{g} (\ddot{r}^{des}_{x} \sin \psi_T - \ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \cos \psi_T), \\
        \theta^{des} &= \frac{1}{g} (\ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \cos \psi_T + \ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \sin \psi_T), \\
        F_B^{des} &= m(\ddot{r}^{des}_{z} + g).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Inner-loop desired moments $\mathbf{M}_B^{des}$ regulate orientation against angular velocities $(p, q, r)$:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}        
        M_{xB}^{des} &= k_{p, \phi} (\phi^{des} - \phi) + k_{d, \phi} (p^{des} - p), \\
        M_{yB}^{des} &= k_{p, \theta} (\theta^{des} - \theta) + k_{d, \theta} (q^{des} - q), \\
        M_{zB}^{des} &= k_{p, \psi} (\psi^{des} - \psi) + k_{d, \psi} (r^{des} - r).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
