\section{System Description}

\subsection{System Modeling}

We define an inertial world frame $\mathcal{W}$ with an upward-pointing $Z$-axis, and a body frame $\mathcal{B}$ anchored at the board's geometric center. Within $\mathcal{B}$, the positive $X$-axis points forward along the vehicle, the positive $Y$-axis points to the left, completing a right-handed coordinate system, and the positive $Z$-axis points upward. At identity orientation (level and unrotated), the axes of $\mathcal{B}$ align with those of $\mathcal{W}$.

The rigid-frame vehicle consists of a $0.8$~kg, $1 \times 1$~m square board with four $1.5$~kg Iris quadrotors \cite{Furrer2016} rigidly mounted at its corners. The four quadrotors are indexed $i \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ and mounted at the board's corners, with quadrotor $i$ at positions $(x_1, y_1) = (0.5, 0.5)$, $(x_2, y_2) = (0.5, -0.5)$, $(x_3, y_3) = (-0.5, 0.5)$, and $(x_4, y_4) = (-0.5, -0.5)$ in $\mathcal{B}$. Each quadrotor $i$ maintains a local frame $\mathcal{B}_i$ that is strictly parallel to $\mathcal{B}$: because the attachment is rigid, there is no relative rotation between $\mathcal{B}_i$ and $\mathcal{B}$, and the two frames differ only by a fixed translation $(x_i, y_i)$ in the board's $XY$ plane.

The vehicle's global position in $\mathcal{W}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{r} = [x, y, z]^T$, and its orientation is parameterized by the Euler angles $\boldsymbol{\eta} = [\phi, \theta, \psi]^T$.

The MAS employs a centralized control architecture derived from \cite{mellinger2013trajectory}. The composite rigid body is commanded via a unified control wrench $\boldsymbol{\tau}$, defined as:

\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} = [F_B, M_{xB}, M_{yB}, M_{zB}]^T,
\end{equation}

\noindent where $F_B$ is the total collective thrust along the board's $Z$-axis, and $M_{xB}$, $M_{yB}$, and $M_{zB}$ are the desired roll, pitch, and yaw moments applied to the board frame $\mathcal{B}$. These relate to the individual quadrotor thrust inputs $\mathbf{u} = [F_1, F_2, F_3, F_4]^T$, where $F_i$ denotes the thrust produced by quadrotor $i$ along its local $Z$-axis (parallel to the board's $Z$-axis), through the allocation matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 4}$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tau}
    \boldsymbol{\tau} = A \mathbf{u},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    A =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
        y_1 & y_2 & y_3 & y_4 \\
        -x_1 & -x_2 & -x_3 & -x_4 \\
        \gamma_1 & \gamma_2 & \gamma_3 & \gamma_4
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

The rows of $A$ correspond to the total thrust, roll moment, pitch moment, and yaw moment on the board, respectively. Here, $(x_i, y_i)$ is the position of quadrotor $i$'s center of thrust expressed in $\mathcal{B}$, and $\gamma_i$ is the yaw moment coefficient (torque-to-thrust ratio) of quadrotor $i$, with its sign dictated by the rotor's spin direction.

\subsection{Dynamic Thrust Allocation}

When a payload shifts from the board's geometric center, it alters the
effective moment arms of each quadrotor. Let $\hat{\mathbf{c}} =
[\hat{c}_x, \hat{c}_y]^T$ denote the estimated system CoG expressed in
$\mathcal{B}$, produced directly by the PINN and fed to the control
allocation matrix. Redefining the lever arms of quadrotor $i$, measured from
the system CoG, as $\Delta x_i = x_i - \hat{c}_x$ and $\Delta y_i = y_i -
\hat{c}_y$, the allocation matrix adapts dynamically:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}_{\text{final}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\
        \Delta y_1 & \Delta y_2 & \Delta y_3 & \Delta y_4 \\
        -\Delta x_1 & -\Delta x_2 & -\Delta x_3 & -\Delta x_4 \\
        \gamma_1 & \gamma_2 & \gamma_3 & \gamma_4
    \end{bmatrix}.
    \label{eq:allocation_matrix}
\end{equation}

\noindent Control inputs are computed via the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse to
handle transient rank deficiencies:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u} = \mathbf{A}_{\text{final}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\tau}.
    \label{eq:final_u}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Centralized Control Design}
Based on \cite{mellinger2013trajectory}, the controller separates position and attitude tracking. Position and velocity errors define commanded acceleration $\mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{cmd}$:

\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{e}_p = \mathbf{r}_{des} - \mathbf{r}, \quad \mathbf{e}_v = \mathbf{\dot{r}}_{des} - \mathbf{\dot{r}},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{cmd} = K_p \mathbf{e}_p + K_d \mathbf{e}_v + \mathbf{\ddot{r}}_{\text{ff}}.
\end{equation}

Desired roll ($\phi^{des}$), pitch ($\theta^{des}$), and total thrust ($F_B^{des}$) are extracted via small-angle approximation:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        \phi^{des} &= \frac{1}{g} (\ddot{r}^{des}_{x} \sin \psi_T - \ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \cos \psi_T), \\
        \theta^{des} &= \frac{1}{g} (\ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \cos \psi_T + \ddot{r}^{des}_{y} \sin \psi_T), \\
        F_B^{des} &= m(\ddot{r}^{des}_{z} + g).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Inner-loop desired moments $\mathbf{M}_B^{des}$ regulate orientation against angular velocities $(p, q, r)$:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}        
        M_{xB}^{des} &= k_{p, \phi} (\phi^{des} - \phi) + k_{d, \phi} (p^{des} - p), \\
        M_{yB}^{des} &= k_{p, \theta} (\theta^{des} - \theta) + k_{d, \theta} (q^{des} - q), \\
        M_{zB}^{des} &= k_{p, \psi} (\psi^{des} - \psi) + k_{d, \psi} (r^{des} - r).
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
