\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation and Related Works}
Cooperative multi-quadrotor systems provide scalable, high-capacity solutions for payload transportation \cite{guebsi_drones_2024, tan_cooperative_2018}. While cable-suspended payloads \cite{zeng_decentralized_2025,peng_modeling_2023} are common, rigidly attaching quadrotors to a common frame allows the distributed system to be controlled as a single composite rigid body \cite{tan_cooperative_2018}. This simplification assumes a symmetrical mass distribution \cite{mellinger2013trajectory, ye_nonlinear_2023}; in practice, uncertain payload Centers of Gravity (CoG) introduce unmodeled disturbance torques that cause severe lateral drift and may lead to a crash \cite{lee_concurrent_2025,farid_digital_2025}.

To mitigate these disturbances, the literature has explored structural hardware optimizations such as canting rotors to increase yaw authority \cite{tan_cooperative_2018}, alongside several algorithmic adaptations. These include classical batch and recursive least-squares \cite{mellinger2013trajectory}, Concurrent Learning (CL) frameworks \cite{lee_concurrent_2025}, and Digital Twin (DT) Model Predictive Control (MPC) \cite{farid_digital_2025}. Building on this body of work, emerging machine learning techniques offer an alternative estimation mechanism for real-time flight stabilization.

This work presents a class of Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) estimator for cooperative, rigid multi-quadrotor payload transport in simulation. We assess the feasibility of using a PINN to mitigate disturbances from an unmodeled, off-center static payload. The estimator identifies unknown payload mass and static spatial shifts over a short-term receding data horizon, driven by an energy-based loss derived from the Euler-Lagrange and Euler-Poincare equations. It is integrated with a small-angle multi-agent controller \cite{mellinger2013trajectory} that receives the updated offsets directly in its control allocation matrix.
